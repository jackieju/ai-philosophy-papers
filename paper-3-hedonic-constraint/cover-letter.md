**Relevance to the Journal.** The paper addresses a gap at the intersection of moral philosophy and information technology that is central to the journal's aims. Autonomous AI agents deployed in 2024–2026 execute multi-step plans with tool authority over real-world effectors, yet no widely adopted safety principle operates at the level of the plan graph—the level at which these systems actually cause harm. The paper proposes the Hedonic Constraint (HC): an action path is impermissible if and only if it structurally requires the production of negative hedonic states in a moral patient as a necessary means to goal achievement. HC is grounded in three converging traditions—the Kantian Formula of Humanity (operationalized as a graph-theoretic property), Popperian negative utilitarianism, and engineering constraint design—and is shown to be decidable in polynomial time via cut-vertex identification over plan DAGs. Four case studies (peacekeeping system, coding agent, medical AI, resource allocator) demonstrate HC's discriminative power where both "do no harm" and welfare maximization fail. The paper engages directly with recent work published in or forthcoming from journals in the journal's domain, including Abiri (2026) on constitutional AI governance, Brophy (2026) on Rawlsian equilibrium for alignment, Cheng (2026) on via negativa alignment, and Nokhiz, Ruwanpathirana and Nissenbaum (2026) on moral self-inconsistency in LLMs.
